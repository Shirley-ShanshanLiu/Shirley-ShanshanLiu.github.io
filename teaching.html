How I mentor

  1. Projects sized to the student. Newer researchers start by reproducing results, auditing data, and stress-testing assumptions; more experienced students build models and work directly with partners.
  2. Clear checkpoints. We agree on milestones up front, so progress — and problems — surface early.
  3. Work someone else can rerun. Code, data steps, and assumptions are written down as we go.
  4. Ownership that grows. Credit and authorship are discussed early, and students take over more of the research decisions as they gain experience.
