Research

Vehicles, buildings, and the grid — planned as one system

Electric vehicles are becoming part of the power system whether or not we plan for it. I build the models and decision tools needed to plan and run vehicles, buildings, and the grid as one system — so that people can still get where they need to go, and homes and schools stay safe, when storms and outages hit.

Approach

Models that earn their place in a decision

I trained as a transportation engineer — network modeling, optimization, and spatial analysis — with a doctoral focus on computational science, and at Michigan I have added physics-informed machine learning. What ties these together is a simple test: a model matters only if it improves a real decision. So I care as much about when a model can be trusted to guide charging, dispatch, or investment as about how accurate it is on paper.

Foundations · Southeast University, 2013–2020

Rooted in transportation planning practice

Before energy systems, I trained in traffic and transportation engineering in Nanjing — designing feeder-bus routes, transit priority for modern trams, and passenger flows through railway hubs — and worked on applied planning projects for provincial and municipal governments. That grounding in how real networks are planned and operated still shapes how I frame energy questions around service to travelers.

  • Transit network design

    A tabu-search route-design model for feeder buses that fill service gaps left by urban rail, tested in Suzhou (Journal of Advanced Transportation, 2020); rural and integrated urban–rural bus networks for Ganzhou and Gaoping.

  • Hubs, trams & accessibility

    Signal-priority schemes for modern trams, transit accessibility to railway hubs from open data, and wayfinding in large railway transfer zones (CICTP 2017, 2019), plus three book chapters on trams, passenger hubs, and graduate training.

  • Policy & evaluation

    A Jiangsu provincial transportation strategy identifying multimodal bottlenecks, and multi-criteria evaluation of 700+ regulatory items with recommendations to regional governments.
